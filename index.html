About the book

Easy to schedule. Easy to talk over. Easy to leave.

You were cast as the good girl at seven. This is thirty days of walking out: one page of thinking, one action a day, and the exact sentences for the conversations you keep avoiding.

You learned to be easy before you could read. Easy to schedule, easy to talk over, easy to leave.

That skill worked. It kept rooms calm and it kept you liked, and it still runs every time somebody's mood shifts. Nobody asks you to do it. You do it before you notice: in the meeting where you soften the sentence, in the group chat where you volunteer, at the table where you pay and say nothing.

The Good Girl Exit is thirty days of undoing that, one action per day.

Week one

Diagnosis. You count what the reflex costs in hours and money, and you name the three moves you make faster than thought: apologise, explain, accommodate.

Weeks two to four

Practice. Voice, refusal, money, and the mirror. Each day gives you one thing to do in a real conversation, the sentence to say, and what to do when it goes badly.

Day 3 is “Hold on, I'm not finished.” Day 23 is saying your number out loud ten times before you say it to anyone with a budget. Day 27 is an account nobody else knows about.

By day 30 you have said no without a paragraph of defence, taken credit for work you did, and survived the four things people say when you stop being convenient.

Questions you might have

The things you're already thinking.

“I'm not a people pleaser.”

You do not have to be. There is one test and it takes four seconds: when did you last do something kind that nobody saw?

“I've read the nice-girl books.”

Most of them tell you to heal a pattern. This one treats it as a part you were cast in at seven, then gives you thirty days of lines to say out loud.

“I don't have time.”

Each day is one page and one action. Four minutes is enough.

“Will people be angry?”

Some of them, for about a year. The book tells you which four sentences they will use and what to say back.
